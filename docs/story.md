# Story — *Manual Override*

> The narrative skin for the game in [game-design.md](game-design.md) and the
> course in [curriculum/](curriculum/). Nothing here changes the hardware or the
> core primitive — it renames the objects, adds the **Firewall / breach** stakes,
> and threads a story spine through Classes 1–3. Tone: a planet learning to
> be quiet again, two reflected worlds sharing one pane of glass, a guardian that
> ran for generations and then went dark, and the slow, earned raising of a ghost
> you can trust.

> *"For a hundred years we shouted into the dark, and something answered. We have
> spent every year since learning to whisper. Pray the Sentinel never falls
> silent — silence is the only wall they cannot hear through."*
> — opening line, Sentinel Operator's Handbook

---

## Logline

Two worlds share one pane of glass. On the **outside**: steel, vacuum, the weight
of your own hand. On the **inside**: the net, and the things climbing out of it.
Neither world can defend itself — the digital wall can only be held by a physical
body reaching in, and the physical station can only be struck from the digital
side. You stand at the one seam between them.

For generations the **Sentinel** has held that wall on its own — an automated
guardian that keeps humanity's radio noise from leaking into the dark, so the things
our noise once woke can't find us again. It worked so long that everyone forgot how.
Then, suddenly, it **stopped.** A lunar relay falls dark under an intrusion no one
can name, the wall starts to leak, and there is no one alive who still remembers how
the Sentinel did its job.

So the wall falls to *you*: a human hand poured into a steel arm, holding the Firewall
by hand because the machine no longer can. But a hand cannot hold it forever — and
every breach screams our location back into the void. Your real mission is to **get
the Sentinel back up**: relearn its job by doing it yourself, then pour what you
learned into a new mind and hand the wall back — before too much noise escapes and
wakes something far worse than what's already at the gate.

## Backstory — the long silence

It started with the Industrial Revolution. The moment we electrified the world we
began **leaking** — radio, radar, broadcast, a century of careless noise spilling off
the planet at the speed of light, a beacon announcing *here we are* to anything
listening. For a long time nothing was. Then the **first curious ones** arrived,
drawn down the trail of our own signal. We barely survived the visit.

What saved us was not a bigger gun. It was **silence.** We built the **Sentinel** — a
grid of guardian stations whose true job is not to shoot but to *contain*: to catch
our outbound noise at the edge and stop it reaching the dark, so the trail goes cold
and the deeper things never wake. For generations it held. The sky went quiet. People
forgot there had ever been anything to fear — and, more dangerously, forgot how the
Sentinel actually worked. It simply ran, and was trusted, and was left alone.

**Selene Station** is one node of that grid — a relay on the far side of the Moon, the
quietest place we know, chosen because the Moon's own body shields it from Earth's
racket. From here the Sentinel listens for what slips through, and seals it.

## The turn — the day it stopped

Then, after all those years, **Selene's Sentinel stopped.** No warning, no clear
fault. The wall it held began to leak; an intrusion no one can name started climbing
through the gaps; and our noise — held back for generations — began bleeding into the
void again, one breach at a time. Somewhere out there, the trail is warming.

And here is the trap the silence set: the Sentinel ran so long, so reliably, that
**no one alive remembers how it does the job.** You cannot just switch it back on —
the mind that knew how is gone. There is no manual for the thing the manual was
supposed to make unnecessary.

So the Station falls back to its oldest, ugliest protocol: **manual override.** A
*human* takes the shell and holds the wall by hand — clumsy, slow, one motion at a
time — sealing breaches before the noise pours out. **Survive the watch**, and you buy
the world one more quiet night. But a hand cannot do this forever, and every night the
Sentinel stays dark, more of our voice escapes. The only way out is the slow one:
**relearn the job by hand, then teach it to a new mind** and bring the Sentinel back —
before the trail gets loud enough to wake what we were hiding from.

## The right order (the moral)

You cannot reboot the Sentinel by flipping a switch, and you cannot rebuild it by
handing it a script. The Station is in this danger *because* of how the old Sentinel
was treated — automation everyone trusted and no one understood, run for so long that
the skill behind it withered, leaving no human who could step in the day it failed.
That is the real lesson of the long silence: **automation you don't understand is a
wall with no one behind it.**

So you do it in the only order that works:

1. **By hand first.** Hold the wall yourself, badly, until you *feel* how it moves.
   You can't automate a thing you've never done.
2. **Then understand.** Give yourself eyes, find the patterns in the Cascade, turn
   wasted motion into strategy. You can't teach what you can't yet explain.
3. **Only then, automate.** Raise the ghost, hand it the shell, and watch — ready to
   override. Automation is the *reward* for understanding, never the shortcut past it.

That is the title. **Manual Override** is not just how you start; it is the right you
keep — the power to take the wall back from the Sentinel at any moment, because you
once held it yourself.

## Two realities, one mirror

This is the spine of the whole fiction: **there are two worlds, they are reflections
of each other, and the glass between them is the only place they touch.** The
hardware *is* that mirror.

- **Outside the glass — meatspace.** The steel arm — your **Shell** — and the **Keys**
  it carries. Slow, heavy, mechanical, real. This is the world a body lives in. Nothing
  in the net can reach across and crush you here; but you can reach *in*.

- **Inside the glass — the Lattice.** What the screen shows is not a display, it is the
  *other side*: the dark our signal escapes into and the curious ones listen from, a
  reflected world falling away into depth with **intrusions** climbing out of it toward
  the **Firewall**. Nothing physical lives here; but everything that threatens the
  Station is born here, and it can only be fought from the outside.

- **The pane itself is the mirror — and it works both ways.** Every **port** exists in
  *both* worlds at once — a point on the glass the Lattice pushes against from within and
  your Shell seals from without. The wall keeps two things apart at once: it holds the
  intrusions *out*, and it holds our noise *in*. So an unsealed port is a door swinging
  both directions — an intruder climbs in **and** a thread of our voice screams out into
  the dark. A **breach** is the two worlds bleeding into each other, and a little more of
  *here we are* leaking toward things best left asleep.

- **The Lens is the rift.** The overhead camera and ArUco tags are the *one* crossing
  between sides — the gate the realities see each other through. The Lattice cannot
  perceive your Shell except through the Lens; the moment a Key rests in a port, the
  Lens carries your physical position across the mirror and the net feels the seal
  close. Lose the Lens and the worlds go blind to each other.

The fantasy, in one line: **you defend one reality with the other.** You hold a digital
wall with a steel hand, because the physical is the one thing the digital can't corrupt
— and the digital is the one place the physical can't be hurt. Each world is the other's
weapon and the other's wound.

The *ghost in the machine* note runs underneath all of it. A ghost is the only thing
that crosses the mirror — a mind, poured out of one world to act in the other. First it
is *your* mind in the Shell. Later it is the Sentinel you wake. And somewhere in the loop of
act-see-remember, something that looks like judgment starts to form on the far side of
the glass — and the whole question of the course is whether you can trust the reflection
looking back, and whether you'd know if you couldn't.

## The new stakes: the glass cracks

This is what the story adds on top of "a point on contact." It turns a score race into
a night you have to *survive*.

- Every port sits behind a **pane of glass** in the Firewall.
- An intrusion reaches its port. If a Key is seated in it at **the dock**, you
  **purge** — the intrusion dissolves, the glass holds, no damage.
- If the dock is unsealed, the intrusion **latches and feeds**: spider-web cracks crawl
  outward from the port, visible and escalating. That's HP coming off — and through every
  crack, a thread of the **Noise** screams out into the dark.
- A Key arriving *late* can still **interrupt the feed** — it stops the bleeding, but the
  cracks already cut into the glass remain.
- **Firewall integrity is your HP.** When a pane fully shatters, that's a **breach**. Lose
  the wall before the watch is over and the Station falls — and a generation of held-back
  Noise floods out at once, lighting a trail straight to us.

> *Optional global stake (co-op / campaign):* track a rising **Noise meter** that ticks up
> with every breach and never fully resets between watches. It's the long game under the
> nightly one — hold the wall well enough, long enough, to bring the Sentinel back *before
> the meter wakes what the curious ones were only the scouts of.*

The dread is diegetic: you can *see* how close the wall is to failing, because it is
cracking in front of you.

## Hybrid rules: defense **is** offense

> This refines the competitive structure in
> [game-design.md §3, §5, §6](game-design.md). Where that doc and this one differ,
> treat this section as canonical for hybrid mode.

Two ghosts, two Shells, two Firewalls, one shared Lattice. Intrusions climb toward
ports on **both** walls.

1. **Defend.** An intrusion at an unsealed dock cracks *your* glass (see above).
2. **Weaponize on purge.** A **clean purge** doesn't just save you — the captured
   intrusion is **turned and fired back**: it spawns a faster **counter-strike** homing
   on a random *opponent* port. Good defense automatically becomes pressure on the other
   ghost. You attack *by defending well*, never by neglecting your wall.
   - *Optional depth:* the **earlier** the seal (a Key already waiting when the intrusion
     docks, vs. one that scrambles in at the last frame), the **stronger** the
     counter-strike. This is what makes pre-staging your 3 Keys pay off, and ties into the
     re-key rotation.
3. **The Seam.** Ports in the overlap can be sealed by *either* ghost, and counter-strikes
   love to target them — the cheapest place to land damage. The safety arbiter still
   forbids both Shells occupying the same space, so the Seam is where you both want to be
   and only one of you can.
4. **Win / lose.**
   - Reduce the **opponent's Firewall integrity to zero → instant win** (their wall
     shatters; their port falls).
   - Otherwise, **survive the watch** and the **higher remaining integrity wins**;
     ties broken by most intrusions purged.

The feedback loop is the drama: the better you hold your wall, the harder you hit theirs,
the more they have to hold — until someone's glass gives.

## Story spine across the course

The narrative *is* the curriculum arc — and it follows the right order: do, understand,
automate.

- **Class 1 — *By hand.*** The Sentinel has stopped; the Cascade is climbing the leaking
  wall; it's yours to hold. You ride the Shell with raw joints, and every motion is a fight
  against the body. **The clumsiness is the story, not a flaw** — this is what holding a
  wall by hand actually feels like, and you need to feel it before you can ever hand it off.
  → [class-1-thinking-in-planes.md](curriculum/class-1-thinking-in-planes.md)

- **Class 2 — *Understand.*** The Shell gains the Lens. You stop fighting the body and
  start *reading the Cascade* — which intrusion to chase, when to re-key, how to hold the
  Seam. The in-fiction lesson lands on its own: at the wall, **a fast specialized sensor
  beats waking the big mind just to look.** Speed is survival — and understanding is what
  you'll need to teach a new ghost what you know.
  → [class-2-vision-and-strategy.md](curriculum/class-2-vision-and-strategy.md)

- **Class 3 — *Automate.*** The Sentinel can't be switched back on — the mind that knew its
  job is gone. So you **rebuild it**: pour what your hand learned into a new mind that acts,
  sees the result through the Lens, and keeps what works. Step by step you hand over the
  shell and watch, ready to override — replacing yourself only as fast as the Sentinel earns
  it, and bringing the wall (and the silence) back online. The course ends on its hardest
  question: **can you trust a mind you raised — and would you know if you couldn't?**
  → [class-3-closing-the-loop.md](curriculum/class-3-closing-the-loop.md)

The [tournament](curriculum/tournament.md) is the proof: a Shell ridden by a human ghost
against a Shell ridden by the Sentinel the students trained — the wall held by hand against
the wall held by a mind that learned. Whose night lasts longer?
